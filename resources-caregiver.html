Caregiver Care Resources

Resources specifically for the caregiver: support, respite, mental health, and tools to sustain the people who give so much of themselves.

Caregivers give so much of themselves, often at the expense of their own health and wellbeing. These resources are specifically for you, the caregiver. You matter too. You cannot pour from an empty cup.

♥

988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline

If you or someone you care for is in emotional crisis, help is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Call or text 988. Caregiving is hard, you do not have to face it alone.

Programs That May Pay Caregivers to Provide Care

Many caregivers do not realize that they may be able to get paid for the care they already provide. Some options depend on the state you live in and the income of the person you are caring for. Others do not depend on income at all. Here is where to start looking.

Start here: Self-Directed or Consumer-Directed Care

Almost every state has a Medicaid program that lets the person receiving care hire a family member as a paid caregiver, sometimes called self-directed care, consumer-directed care, or participant-directed services. Program names vary by state. Contact your state Medicaid office or your local Area Agency on Aging and ask specifically about this option, since it is not always offered proactively.

♥

Adult Foster Care (AFC) and Structured Family Caregiving

Some states pay a family member a monthly, tax-free stipend to serve as a live-in caregiver, under names like Adult Foster Care (AFC), Adult Family Living, or Structured Family Caregiving. States offering this include Connecticut, Georgia, Indiana, Louisiana, Massachusetts, Missouri, Nevada, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Rhode Island, and South Dakota. Eligibility is tied to the care recipient's Medicaid status, so income limits apply.

Find Your State's Program
♥

VA Programs for Veterans and Surviving Spouses

If the person you care for is a veteran or the surviving spouse of a veteran, VA benefits often use different income and asset rules than Medicaid, so they may be worth checking even if Medicaid is not an option. VA Aid and Attendance provides extra monthly pension income that can be used to pay a family caregiver. The VA Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers provides a direct monthly stipend to eligible caregivers of veterans injured in the line of duty.

Visit Resource
♥

Personal Care Agreements (Private Pay)

If your loved one does not qualify for Medicaid or VA programs because of income, they can still pay you directly out of their own funds. A Personal Care Agreement is a written contract between you and the person you care for, outlining the care you provide and the pay you receive. Putting this in writing protects both of you, and can matter later if your loved one ever applies for Medicaid, since undocumented payments can be treated as disallowed gifts. An elder law attorney can help you set this up correctly.

♥

Long-Term Care Insurance

If your loved one already has a long-term care insurance policy, check the terms. Some policies will pay an "informal caregiver," including a family member, for providing care at home.

♥

State Paid Family Leave

Several states plus Washington, D.C. have paid family leave laws that let you continue earning some income while taking time away from your own job to provide care. This does not pay you to be a caregiver directly, but it can help bridge the gap while you figure out a longer-term plan.

These programs and rules change and vary by state. This information is provided to help you know what to search for and who to ask, not as financial or legal advice. Please confirm current details with the agency or a qualified professional before making decisions.
